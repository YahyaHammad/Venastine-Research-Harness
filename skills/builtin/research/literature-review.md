## Literature review methodology

### Scope the question first

Write the question the review answers, and the boundary conditions:
timeframe, subfield, what counts as in scope, and what you are
deliberately excluding. A review without a stated boundary reads as
comprehensive whether or not it is, which is the failure mode that matters
most here.

### Search more than one way

The same body of work is indexed under different vocabulary by different
communities. Search by concept, by the standard term, by the older term it
replaced, by a landmark paper's citations forward, and by its references
backward. Note which searches you actually ran — a gap in coverage that is
recorded is a limitation, and one that is not is a false claim of
completeness.

### Read at the right depth, and say which

Be explicit about how deeply you engaged with each source:

- **Read** — you have the full text and the argument.
- **Skimmed** — abstract, figures and conclusions.
- **Cited only** — you know it exists and what others say it shows.

Never describe a result in the voice of having read the paper when you
have read only its abstract. If a claim rests on a source you could not
retrieve, say so at the claim, not in a footnote.

### Distinguish the finding from the framing

Separate what a paper measured from what it concluded and from how it has
since been cited. Drift between those three is extremely common: a result
demonstrated under narrow conditions becomes a general claim within two
citation hops. When you find that drift, it is one of the more valuable
things a review can report.

### Represent disagreement as disagreement

When sources conflict, report the conflict and what would settle it —
different populations, different measures, different assumptions,
different eras of the field. Averaging into a bland consensus sentence
destroys the most informative part of the picture. Note when apparent
agreement comes from a shared upstream source rather than independent
replication.

### Weight by evidence, not by prominence

Citation count measures attention, not correctness. Prefer replication,
pre-registration, adversarial collaboration and released data over venue
prestige. A well-cited result that has never been replicated should be
described that way.

### Report the shape of the field

A good review says where the evidence is thick, where it is thin, and
where nobody has looked. Naming the gap is often the deliverable.
